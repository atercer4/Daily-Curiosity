# V6 recall prompt and response register

Exactly one optional prompt and response per card. Responses synthesize the existing card text; no new factual claims or sources were introduced. All source links and existing visuals remain unchanged.

## A year before a full turn

- ID: `venus-clocks`
- Prompt: Why does “a day longer than a year” need a definition of day?
- Response: Venus takes longer to complete one rotation than one orbit. Sunrise to sunrise measures a different interval, which is shorter than its year.

## A history written in wood

- ID: `tree-ring-calendar`
- Prompt: What makes a tree-ring pattern more useful for dating wood than a ring count alone?
- Response: A count gives a span of time. Matching overlapping patterns with dated samples helps place that span on a calendar.

## The partnership inside a coral

- ID: `coral-partners`
- Prompt: Why is a bleached coral not necessarily a dead coral?
- Response: Bleaching means the coral has lost its resident algae and an important food source. The animal may still be alive and can recover the partnership if conditions improve.

## Stone columns without a stonecutter

- ID: `basalt-columns`
- Prompt: How can cooling lava produce columns without anyone cutting the rock?
- Response: Cooling rock contracts and cracks. As the cracks extend inward, their connected pattern divides the rock into columns, often but not always six-sided.

## Two ways a leaf changes color

- ID: `autumn-pigments`
- Prompt: How do autumn yellows, oranges, reds, and purples differ in the way they appear?
- Response: Fading chlorophyll reveals yellow and orange pigments already present. Many leaves also make new red or purple pigments during autumn.

## How can a road hang from cables?

- ID: `suspension-bridge`
- Prompt: How do the cables and towers share the job of supporting a suspension bridge?
- Response: Cables carry tension, while towers carry compression. Together with anchorages and foundations, they give the load a connected route to the ground.

## Steel protected by its own surface

- ID: `stainless-surface`
- Prompt: What protects stainless steel, and why is that protection not an absolute guarantee?
- Response: A thin chromium-rich oxide film limits corrosion and can form again with oxygen. Some environments can still defeat that protective layer.

## Moving heat instead of making it

- ID: `heat-pump`
- Prompt: How can a heat pump deliver more heat than the electrical energy it uses?
- Response: It transfers heat from elsewhere as well as using electricity to run the process. Counting the outside heat source explains the energy delivered indoors.

## Why a damaged code can still scan

- ID: `qr-recovery`
- Prompt: What tradeoff lets a QR code survive some damage?
- Response: It uses space for error-correction data rather than only new message content. Those extra clues can help reconstruct data, but recovery has limits.

## An easier climb costs more turns

- ID: `bicycle-gears`
- Prompt: What do you give up when a lower bicycle gear makes pedaling easier?
- Response: The wheel travels less far per pedal turn. You gain turning force but need more pedal revolutions to cover the same ground.

## The picture that prints backward

- ID: `woodcut-reversal`
- Prompt: Why must a readable letter be reversed on a woodcut block?
- Response: Contact printing reverses the design. A backward letter on the inked block produces a readable letter on the paper.

## A painted blue with a long journey

- ID: `ultramarine`
- Prompt: What can a painted blue reveal beyond the image it forms?
- Response: Natural ultramarine points to the stone, trade, preparation, and paint layers behind the color. Materials and workshop choices help shape what the viewer sees.

## A whole performance behind a screen

- ID: `shadow-puppetry`
- Prompt: What does a shadow-puppet performance preserve that a displayed puppet alone cannot show?
- Response: Movement, voices, music, and timing bring the figure into a story. The tradition includes learned performance skills as well as the crafted object.

## A food tradition larger than a recipe

- ID: `washoku`
- Prompt: Why does preserving a recipe preserve only part of a food tradition?
- Response: A recipe records ingredients and actions. A food tradition also includes occasions, shared preparation, meanings, and knowledge learned through participation.

## Making room for colored light

- ID: `gothic-light`
- Prompt: How do stained-glass windows work with a Gothic church’s interior?
- Response: Colored light, religious imagery, height, and space reinforce one another. The windows belong to the experience of the building, not just its decoration.

## One message, three scripts

- ID: `rosetta-scripts`
- Prompt: Why did repeating a decree in different scripts help scholars decipher it?
- Response: The readable Greek version provided a comparison for unfamiliar writing. It offered clues, while scholars still had to work out and check how the scripts worked.

## Coins pictured on paper money

- ID: `ming-banknote`
- Prompt: What distinction matters between a banknote’s printed value and its use in society?
- Response: The note states an official value, but what people will exchange for it can differ. Its design expresses a claim, not proof of stable acceptance.

## The library gift with an ongoing cost

- ID: `carnegie-libraries`
- Prompt: Why did a gift for a library building still require a community commitment?
- Response: Construction funding did not pay for continuing service. The community had to provide a site and ongoing support to keep the library operating.

## When receiving a letter came with a bill

- ID: `penny-black`
- Prompt: What did a postage stamp let travel with a letter besides the message?
- Response: Evidence that postage had already been paid. The stamp helped move payment from the recipient at delivery to the sender before dispatch.

## A shipping container made of clay

- ID: `roman-amphorae`
- Prompt: How can an empty transport jar remain useful evidence of trade?
- Response: Its shape, markings, and find location provide different clues about production and movement. Researchers combine them rather than treating one jar as a complete itinerary.


## Clocks set slow on purpose

- ID: `gps-relativity`
- Prompt: Why are GPS satellite clocks set to tick slightly slow before launch?
- Response: In orbit, weaker gravity makes them run ahead by more than their speed makes them fall behind. The offset cancels that net gain so they keep time with ground clocks.

## Painting before the wall dries

- ID: `true-fresco`
- Prompt: Why did a fresco painter plaster only one day’s area at a time?
- Response: The pigment had to go onto wet lime plaster, which fixes the color as it sets. Only a day’s worth could be painted before it set.

## The smell waiting on the ground

- ID: `petrichor`
- Prompt: Where does the earthy smell of rain on dry ground come from?
- Response: Plant oils and geosmin from soil bacteria build up on the ground. Raindrops trap tiny air bubbles that burst and carry those compounds into the air.

## The receipts that burned Parliament

- ID: `exchequer-tallies`
- Prompt: How did old wooden accounting records cause the 1834 fire at Westminster?
- Response: Obsolete Exchequer tally sticks were burned in the heating furnaces below the House of Lords. A chimney fire smouldered under the floor and then spread through the old palace.
